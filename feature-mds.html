How this differs from the exclusive families

The family tree divides the complete vocabulary into 16 mutually exclusive families. Every word belongs to exactly one leaf. The 544 features here overlap: a word can carry a prefix, an interior construction, a suffix, and an ordered combination at once.

For example, qokedy and chedy share the leaf “interior e; no eo; has ed; ends in edy.” chol and chor share a different leaf: “no interior e; no a; no ee; has ch.” That groups words by construction; it does not establish a transformation between the groups.

Each family’s gradual volume sums probabilities from the whole gradual model, including every overlapping gradual effect and the baseline weights affecting its members. The separate jump terms are omitted. The 16 volumes therefore sum to 10,000 at each bifolium. But a family can contain multiple, even opposing, movements: these summaries do not preserve all 544 trends separately.

The tree selects up to four successive presence/absence splits using variation in fitted within-family shares, with at least 100 observed tokens on each side. It is a descriptive summary, not an independent test that 16 historical processes existed. Inspect the tree and its volumes.

Metric SMACOF MDS, best of four starts. Level curves are mean-centered and normalized to unit length; slope curves are normalized without centering, preserving the distinction between increases and decreases. Each observed bifolium receives equal weight in the comparison.

Distance rank correlation measures how well the map preserves the ordering of full-curve distances. Neighbor recall is the share of each feature’s ten closest full-curve neighbors also among its ten nearest map points. Relative distance error is the RMS distance discrepancy divided by the RMS original distance; it is not a predictive error or variance explained.
