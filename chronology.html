Method notes

The joint model gives every spelling a fitted probability at every bifolium. Its total movement between neighbouring bifolia locates the steps where the vocabulary changes most. The twelve component groups and the sixteen exclusive families then show, on the same step axis, which movement patterns and which parts of the vocabulary carry each peak.

Numbered peaks are the steps where many spellings change probability at once.

The Columns control above switches every timing matrix between one column per bifolium (the fitted slope or level at each bifolium) and one column per step (the change between consecutive bifolia). Groups overlap, so a word can contribute to several rows.

The family matrix is a complete accounting of where the fitted vocabulary volume sits, or moves, without counting a word twice.

Movement is total variation (half the summed absolute difference) between adjacent gradual word distributions; peaks are local maxima with prominence at least 8% of the largest step, at least two steps apart. Columns are equally spaced bifolia or steps in path order, chosen once for all pages with the Columns control; the fit itself used the geometric path coordinate, which the slope view expresses. Order and geometry are assumed, missing quires can hide intermediate change, and a peak is not evidence of an instantaneous historical event.
