01

The gradient

The gradient is the fitted change in each construction's rate at each step along the proposed order, in tokens per 10,000 words.

  • Each value has two parts. The gradual change comes from a smooth curve. The signed jump is the level difference at that bifolium that the smooth curve could not follow, absorbed by a step term. It is mostly bifolium-specific variation; only a jump that persists suggests a real shift.
  • The gradient matrix is this table: 544 constructions by 49 steps on Path 1, one colour per cell. Teal rises, orange falls.
  • Example: suffix:edy rises from 31 per 10,000 at the first bifolium to 1,742 at the last.
  • Its largest gradual step is +843 into H:57-66 (entering 3b); its largest jump is +545 into M:76-83.
  • Example: cho falls from 2,216 to 107 per 10,000; its largest step is −335 into O:87-90 (2a).
  • The other pages group these values, partition the words, time them, audit them, or apply them to text.
02

The model

  • The manuscript has 34,977 readable word tokens, 7,180 distinct spellings, 207 pages and 50 bifolia. A bifolium is one folded sheet of four pages.
  • The model gives every spelling a probability at every bifolium. The probabilities sum to one, so a spelling gains share only if others lose it.
  • A spelling's probability is set by the constructions it contains (section 04). Each construction has a fixed weight, a gradual curve along the order and, where the curve misses a bifolium, a step term.
  • Gradual curves are penalised: 544 of 1,324 constructions got one on Path 1, with 42 steps. The rest still move through overlapping constructions.
  • A construction's carrier rate is the summed probability of the spellings containing it, per 10,000 words.
  • The gradual level is the carrier rate from the smooth curve alone. Gradual + jumps adds the step terms. The residual is the observed rate minus gradual + jumps.
03

The order, and the two x axes

  • Path 1 is the proposed sequence of the 50 bifolia, from an earlier composition study (the Golf study). Path 3 is an alternative sequence for sensitivity checks.
  • That study also placed each bifolium on a geometric curve. Its position on the curve, from 0 to 1, is the path coordinate. The model was fitted on that coordinate.
  • The coordinate is very unevenly spaced. The last five bifolia, all 4a, cover 0.1% of it. Some neighbouring bifolia in the middle are far apart.
  • So the same 50 bifolia can be drawn two ways. Index spacing gives every bifolium one equal column (upper row). Path spacing places it by its coordinate (lower row).
  • All charts and matrices use index spacing. The shaded bands are the eleven Golf sections, 1a to 4a: blocks of the sequence named by the same study.
  • On the index axis there are two kinds of column.
  • Bifolia: one column per bifolium, showing a value there, such as the fitted slope or level.
  • Steps: one column per change between consecutive bifolia (49 steps), labelled by the destination bifolium.
  • The Columns control switches between them on the matrix, groups and chronology pages, and stays set across pages.
  • A slope divides change by path distance, so near-tied bifolia give steep slopes. The Steps view shows the change itself.
04

Constructions

A construction is a yes-or-no rule about a spelling. The spellings that match it are its carrier words; their tokens are its carrier tokens. Older pages say “component” for construction.

anywhere
a glyph sequence anywhere in the word: eo, cho
prefix · suffix
a sequence at the start or end of a longer word: prefix:qok, suffix:edy, y$
interior
a sequence with glyphs on both sides of it: interior:e, interior:ok
ordered pair
two spans immediately following one another: interior:ok >> suffix:edy
co-occurrence
two patterns both present, in any position

Constructions overlap heavily. qokedy carries prefix:qok, suffix:edy, interior:ok, e and many more, so its probability combines all their weights.

Component groups versus exclusive families

Two different ways of grouping. Groups collect curves and overlap; families partition the words and do not.

06

Reading the charts and matrices

  • Teal means an increase, or a value above the row's usual level; orange a decrease or below; white no change.
  • One colour scale is shared by all displayed rows and saturates at the 95th percentile of absolute values.
  • Gradual change plus signed jump equals the combined change at each step. A jump is a local level difference absorbed by a step term, not a slope; treat it as unexplained bifolium variation unless it persists.
  • Row order “Ward” places similar movement shapes together; “Movement timing” sorts rows early to late by the midpoint of their cumulative change.
  • The Chronology page measures how far the whole fitted vocabulary moves at each step and numbers the peaks. The same numbers are drawn through the group and family timing matrices.
  • Hovering a cell gives the exact value; in the gradient matrix it also gives the raw page counts.

08

Caveats

  • Everything is conditional on the proposed order and geometry, which are assumed, not tested.
  • Missing quires (gatherings of bifolia) can hide intermediate stages. A peak between two surviving bifolia is not evidence of a sudden event.
  • The model describes how the mix of spellings differs along the order. It does not show which spelling became which, that constructions act independently, or that meaning was kept.
  • Groups and families are descriptive cuts chosen on these data, without significance tests. Held-page checks are repeatability checks, not forecasts.
